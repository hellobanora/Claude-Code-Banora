# AHPRA advertising rules for Banora social content

The compliance-qa agent fails any post that breaks a numbered rule below. The writer reads this before drafting.

Status: rules 1 to 8 are a working summary drafted by Claude and have not been checked against the current AHPRA advertising guidelines. James to review and correct. The "From CLAUDE.md" section is copied from the project's own rules.

## Numbered rules (QA quotes these numbers)

1. No testimonials or anything that works as one: patient quotes, stories, reviews, star ratings, or "our patients say". This is stricter than CLAUDE.md, which only rules out testimonials implying guaranteed outcomes.
2. No claim or implication that treatment will produce a result: cure, fix, heal, eliminate, guaranteed, "get rid of".
3. No health claim that is not well supported by evidence, and no claim that chiropractic treats specific diseases (diabetes, cancer and the like) or conditions outside musculoskeletal care.
4. Nothing that creates an unreasonable expectation of benefit, or encourages people to seek care they may not need ("everyone should get checked", fear, urgency, countdowns).
5. No comparative or superlative claims: best, leading, number one, safest, world-class.
6. No use of "specialist" or any title the practitioner is not registered to use.
7. No offer, discount or gift without its terms and conditions stated.
8. No unresolved placeholder such as [CONFIRM: ...].

## From CLAUDE.md

Never write:
- "We can cure..." or "We will fix..."
- "Guaranteed results" or any outcome promise
- Testimonials that imply guaranteed outcomes
- Claims that chiropractic treats specific diseases (diabetes, cancer, etc.)
- "Our chiropractors are the best in..."
- Before/after comparisons that imply guaranteed outcomes

Always write:
- "May help with...", "Can assist in managing...", "Many patients find relief from..."
- "Results vary between individuals" where a benefit is discussed
- "We recommend a consultation to assess your individual needs"
- Reference to evidence-based care where appropriate

Red flag check before finalising:
1. Does this make a therapeutic claim? Add "may" or "can help".
2. Does this promise a specific outcome? Remove or soften.
3. Does this use a testimonial to imply results? Remove.
