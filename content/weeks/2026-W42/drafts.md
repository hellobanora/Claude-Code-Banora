### Caption

There's no single "perfect" way to sit at a desk.

Plenty of us grew up being told to sit up straight and stay there. Research suggests it's less simple than that: no one position has been shown to be the right one for everybody, and holding any posture for hours tends to get uncomfortable, even a textbook one.

What seems to matter more is changing it up. Shift your weight. Stand when the phone rings. Regular movement through the day may help you feel more comfortable at your desk.

One easy prompt: stand up every time you finish a call.

Save this for your next long desk day, and share it with someone who sits a lot.

## Newsletter blurb

- **Topic:** Desk posture: what matters more than a "perfect" position (Monday's post)
- **Suggested heading:** There's no perfect way to sit

Most of us were told at some point to sit up straight and stay that way. The picture turns out to be less tidy. Research suggests no single sitting posture has been shown to suit everyone. And most people find that holding any position for hours, even a very upright one, gets uncomfortable.

So rather than chasing the perfect setup, try changing things more often. Lean back for a while. Sit forward. Stand for a phone call, or walk to the kitchen between tasks. Moving regularly through the day may help you feel more comfortable at your desk, and it's a lot easier to keep up than sitting like a statue.

If you want one habit to start with, stand up each time you finish a call.

We've put the same idea into a short carousel on Instagram and Facebook this week, if you'd like something to save.
